# Evidence Scenarios

Load only for evidence-heavy pages. Choose the relevant branch and keep the core workflow industry-neutral.

## Shared Rule

Evidence pages must communicate why the audience should believe the message. Use source-appropriate objects, preserve verifiable facts, and avoid invented evidence-like detail.

When fixing fabricated details:

1. Remove or abstract only the unverifiable part.
2. Preserve useful composition, material contrast, hierarchy, and semantic atmosphere.
3. Regenerate the page if the repair becomes flat, repetitive, oversized, or template-like.

## Policy, Standards, And Official Guidance

- Use restrained document summaries, timeline nodes, source labels, abstract headers, paper layering, and institutional material cues.
- Lock confirmed policy names, dates, organizations, and supplied quotations.
- Reject invented seals, emblems, document numbers, signatures, body paragraphs, quotations, or source claims.
- Do not require empty white sheets. Keep non-text paper texture, framing, layering, and subtle document structure.

## Product And Software Proof

- Use real screenshots, UI details, product photos, or workflow outputs as the primary evidence.
- Keep supplied images recognizable. Allow restrained crop, perspective, shadow, and local enlargement.
- Reject fake UI, invented metrics, unreadable screenshot collages, and generic icons replacing real proof.

## Data And Research Evidence

- Use real charts, tables, figures, excerpts, or supplied numeric summaries.
- Lock axes, values, units, dates, source names, and cited conclusions when provided.
- Reject invented values, fake trends, decorative charts, unsupported KPI cards, and fabricated citations.

## Case Study And Outcome Evidence

- Use supplied photos, before-and-after views, deliverables, quotes, or outcome summaries.
- Lock names, dates, locations, metrics, and quotes only when provided.
- Reject generic stock photos presented as proof, invented testimonials, and unsupported performance claims.

## Media, Report, And Source History

- Use restrained source panels, publication covers, headline excerpts, report layers, or chronological evidence objects.
- Preserve supplied publication names, dates, and excerpts.
- Reject invented headlines, logos, quotations, authors, and publication metadata.
