# Table Visual Art Direction

Use for table-led slide images alongside the existing page-guide, prompt-contract, visual-core and QA instructions. Keep the current source, decisions, real-image roles, review gates and output workflow. This guidance changes design execution and visual judgment; it adds no user approval step or fixed table template.

## Start with meaning and the whole page

Identify the actual reading task and relationships: exact lookup, comparison, sequence, hierarchy, ranking, shared values, exceptions or a supported conclusion. Preserve required wording, source order and row/column/span associations under the current content-handling rules. A specification list may have no winning value. Do not invent importance, categories, capabilities or engineering relationships to make a design more dramatic.

Choose one whole-page art direction from the subject, audience, confirmed palette, expressiveness, composition and active assets before styling individual components. Resolve the relationship between the information surface, product/image, background and any scene through compatible material, brightness, geometry, scale, light and spatial anchoring. Sharing a hue alone does not establish coherence.

Scene depth, foreground/background decisions, explicit scene notes and supporting/atmospheric real images can justify a physical carrier or richer spatial integration. With no such cue or contextual need, start with flat graphic/editorial organization or modest surface depth. Neither physical monitors nor floating cards are defaults. A deliberate 2D overlay can work in a photographic scene when its contrast, edges, scale and graphic language belong to the whole composition. Preserve the selected real-image role rather than imposing a universal area quota.

## Choose the composition before the table shell

Treat the source table as an information contract, not an automatic rectangular composition. Preserve required header wording, cell associations, source reading order and genuine spans, while choosing the page's spatial organization from the reader task and confirmed decisions. A strict comparison may need an uninterrupted matrix; a short specification lookup may admit an open editorial register or another integrated organization. Do not fragment a matrix when doing so weakens comparison, or rearrange source items merely to obtain a dramatic silhouette.

Before specifying frames, gradients or textures, make compact internal structural sketches of materially different suitable organizations. These may be words and simple block outlines in the existing guide; do not render extra slide images or add an approval gate. Compare their overall silhouette, first/second reading stops, lookup path, long-copy capacity, and relationship to active assets. Select the structure for those effects. Differences confined to color, corner treatment, backing material or line style are not different structural ideas. Keep this proportional to the page; do not impose a fixed number of alternatives or require a nontraditional form where a matrix serves the meaning best.

Compose information, product and any selected scene together. Object contour, whitespace, plane boundaries and reading zones can shape each other instead of becoming a finished table plus an independent photograph. Physical depth is conditional on the current context; an open graphic register can coexist with photographic depth without pretending to be a screen or hologram. An asset's specified dominance still applies. Do not connect specifications to physical buttons, ports or controls without source evidence, or invent categories, rankings and process relationships to justify the composition.

Record why the chosen structure serves the page better than the suitable alternatives and which visible organization should survive when small text is blurred. During QA, judge the effect rather than the presence of a technique: where the eye lands, how it enters and tracks the information, what the header actually organizes, whether color separates useful roles, and whether the product/scene participates in the composition. Gradients, hierarchy, depth and line removal are means, not completion criteria. For an expressive page, mark visual design unresolved when the result is only a conventional table with cosmetic effects and an adjacent image despite a planned structural mechanism. Do not solve that failure by adding more surface decoration; revise the spatial relationship, retaining content and confirmed decisions. A nontraditional silhouette alone is equally insufficient if it damages lookup or becomes arbitrary display.


## Carry context into the table components

Preserving a row/column matrix does not require preserving conventional spreadsheet-shaped components. After selecting the information structure, derive a coherent component language from the page's subject, audience, expression mode, active scene and assets. Apply that language to horizontal/group headers, vertical identifiers, data surfaces, shared fields and separators, not only to a backing panel or nearby photograph. Explain how their geometry, typographic identity, surface behavior and color roles belong to the same page while retaining distinct reading functions. Merely assigning a fill to each semantic column is not sufficient evidence of this design work.

Choose the degree of physical treatment explicitly from current context. With justified scene/object treatment, components can echo appropriate construction, material, edge behavior and illumination, without pretending to be verified device controls or obscuring text with perspective. With graphic/editorial treatment, use compatible geometry, spacing, color temperature, contrast and type rhythm; a 2D table need not imitate a physical object to belong beside photography. Hybrid treatment may mix those approaches intentionally. Do not default to metallic plaques, cut corners, bevels, blue headers, rounded backplates, no-grid layouts or any other recurring motif. Subject/scene correspondence is a design rationale, not permission to copy an example's shapes or make every element dimensional.

In the existing guide, record the selected treatment mode and concrete contextual choices for the relevant table roles, including how header identity differs from identifiers and plain data. Carry those choices into the actual prompt. QA the internal table independently of the surrounding scene: temporarily ignore the product/background and identify which visible component relationships express this page's chosen language, then check their coherence with the whole page again. A conventional matrix can pass through well-designed contextual components; it need not adopt an unusual silhouette. Conversely, correct grouping, colored columns, a textured backing or a realistic background cannot on their own establish completion. Mark the planned component treatment unresolved if it has collapsed into generic filled cells, or if rich local styling feels incompatible with the page. Repair the failed language/relationship rather than prescribing more effects or banning grids. Readability and truthful data scope remain controlling constraints.


## Translate meaning into specific visual choices

Consider materially different organizations when the page's expressive ambition calls for them, then select one internally. Derive the choice from this page rather than reusing a previous canvas. Poster, editorial, commerce and game-interface design can inform transferable principles such as object/attribute relationships, typography, grouping, material and rhythm; they do not prescribe genre styling, shapes or decorations.

Coordinate the following roles:

- Horizontal headers establish column identity and genuine group hierarchy. Design grouping, type, position, surface, shape and relationship to the data. Parent and child headers can differ. Do not rely on a generic filled strip as the entire design decision.
- Vertical headers and row identifiers establish lookup and row tracking. Distinguish their identity from descriptions through appropriate typography, spacing, grouping or surface treatment. Keep peer roles coherent without mechanically giving every label a bright button.
- Data copy remains comfortably readable but usually calmer and smaller than header/index roles. Choose actual sizes from viewing distance, wording and density; do not impose one size ordering between horizontal and vertical headers. Use regular or medium body weight where appropriate. Short quantities need not become oversized KPIs. Preserve precise identifiers, units and qualifiers.
- Color participates in page composition and information relationships. Assign the confirmed color system to background, carrier, headers, index, data, groups and legitimate emphasis, including lightness, saturation, temperature, area and material color. A hue can identify a real group or shared field; do not highlight every number or create a winner by color alone. Keep local richness compatible with the whole-page art direction.
- Alignment, spacing, surface fields, grouping, type and lines jointly organize rows and columns. Full grids, selective rules, open rows, column planes and mixed treatments are choices. Neither removing all lines nor using a frame around every cell is a universal improvement. Preserve readable correspondence and explicit merged-cell scope.
- Image and graphic detail should clarify the actual object or relationship. Integrate current real content assets according to their roles and bindings. Do not add fake charts, unsupported status/rating encodings, decorative controls or callouts that imply unverified physical functionality.

Material, gradients, local light and depth should positively support those choices and the selected expressiveness. Avoid reducing the page to neutral cells through prohibitions alone. Equally, bevels, skewed tags, glow, background drama or a sophisticated carrier do not independently prove that the table's internal design is complete.

## Record executable design commitments

Within the existing table decision record in the page guide, keep a compact account of:

1. Whole-page art direction and how the table, assets and scene belong together; explain the selected degree of scene/physical treatment.
2. Chosen visual mechanisms with their source-grounded reason. Describe concrete component relationships and treatments, not only adjectives or forbidden items.
3. Expected visible evidence and a failure signal for each important mechanism. For example, genuine column grouping should be apparent in the designed hierarchy; a shared field should visibly retain its complete span. These are examples of reasoning, not required visual forms.
4. Text roles, readable widths/wrapping and image dominance, with source/decision constraints preserved.

Carry those actual choices into the exact saved tool prompt. Check the prompt against the record before generation. A claimed design commitment that is absent from the prompt has not been executed. Keep the record short and request-local; do not add another approval document or prescribed number of alternatives/mechanisms.

## Judge visual completion independently

Use the existing QA flow, but distinguish evidence for:

- Content fidelity: required copy, values, qualifiers, ordering and cell/span relationships.
- Information layout: legibility at presentation size, tracking, alignment, spacing, collisions, title safety and selected image role.
- Visual design: whole-page contextual coherence first, then the planned header/index/data identities, color composition, rhythm, material and meaningful visual mechanisms.

At thumbnail scale inspect the intended visual organization and balance; at presentation scale inspect text comfort and row/column interpretation. Name what is actually visible. Correct data, valid dimensions and repaired spans establish neither visual distinction nor completion of the art direction. Conversely, a rich scene or physical carrier cannot compensate for unresolved internal table design.

If a planned visual mechanism is absent, generic or inconsistent with the whole page, mark that item fail or unresolved and follow failure-repair.md. Repair the failed design relationship while retaining treatments actually checked and passed; recheck both visual and content gates after regeneration. A retry limit does not turn failure into success. Do not claim that an instruction update alone demonstrates improved design, or that internal QA determines the user's aesthetic preference. Report the concrete result and its practical limitations.
