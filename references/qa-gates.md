# QA Gates

Use after generating a body image and again after the title overlay. Run both compliance and visual QA. Passing compliance alone is not a visual approval.

## Compliance QA

Check each body image and final overlaid image:

- It is 16:9 and generated by the official conversational image-generation tool with only allowed title overlay afterward.
- Required real images entered generation as actual visual inputs or shown references and remain recognizable.
- If a real-image slot was left empty under `real_image_policy: skip_when_missing`, the page does not invent fake-real evidence to fill it.
- Locked facts remain correct. Reject invented names, source details, official marks, document identifiers, signatures, quotations, logos, watermarks, statistics, or other evidence-like claims.
- Tier A text appears with acceptable completeness and meaning. Reject pages that sacrifice required information for a cleaner or more stylized composition when the page role is information-bearing.
- When a model invents unverifiable evidence detail, remove or abstract only that detail. Preserve useful composition, material depth, and evidence structure when regenerating.
- Locked text is not obviously corrupted.
- Chinese text does not show high-impact glyph deformation: horizontal squeezing, vertical stretching, condensed/narrow fake font behavior, perspective distortion in body copy, sticky strokes, or pseudo-text replacing required content.
- No watermark, unrelated logo, unrelated stock imagery, fake chart, or fake KPI appears.

## Visual QA

Check each body image and final overlaid image:

- The page has a clear primary visual system, reading path, and meaningful depth.
- The page has one memorable visual anchor. Reject pages that are tidy but visually forgettable.
- The primary visual anchor carries the page's main argument, not only the topic atmosphere. Reject pages where a control room, device, dashboard, city, data tunnel, or other relevant scene becomes the strongest subject while the actual argument, gap, comparison, demand, product definition, or evidence chain remains a set of detached labels.
- The page's information relationship is visible. Reject pages that do not make the confirmed relationship legible, such as cause-effect, contrast gap, supply-demand pressure, system hierarchy, capability decomposition, evidence chain, process path, or comparison matrix.
- For market, demand, strategy, opportunity, and problem pages, reject scene-dominant outputs where atmosphere outranks the business logic. A relevant environment may support the message, but the gap, pressure, chain, contrast, or opportunity window must be the S/A-level visual system.
- The selected primary emphasis method is respected. For `reading-priority`, reject pages that merely make the primary group the largest area while weakening reading order, text fidelity, or visual hierarchy. For `area-priority`, check that the main region has enough area. For `visual-anchor`, check that the anchor is memorable without automatically taking the upper-left first reading position or changing source order. For `path-priority`, check that the path, connectors, and grouping are clear.
- Information priority is not the same as area priority. Reject pages where `group_weights.primary` was translated into a crude full-canvas block or equal-card grid when the decision called for reading priority, visual-anchor priority, or path priority.
- Information appears as semantic visual objects rather than generic PPT components. Reject pages where the main information groups are only boxes, icons, and text without object, path, scene, or material identity.
- If three to six repeated modules appear, their visual treatment must match their role. Reject pages where main modules are under-designed into a plain row/grid, and reject pages where auxiliary modules are over-designed into a competing visual stage.
- Main repeated modules must form a shared visual system rather than a plain row or grid. Reject main-module pages where the modules lack a shared stage, path, cockpit, map, platform, wall, hub, or other system anchor.
- Reject full-screen, flat, evenly weighted module tiling when the intended page should have a product anchor + function list, definition panel + capability console, path diagram, evidence stage, or other composition relationship. High expressiveness must not be accepted when it only adds more borders, glow, badges, particles, or generic grid lines.
- Main repeated modules should include a meaningful second layer when the main row feels thin: direction, state, role, input/output, phase, risk, result, evidence type, or conclusion. Reject second layers that are only decorative labels, badges, or filler.
- Main repeated modules should show local rhythm inside shared grammar. Reject pages where each main module has identical size, icon strength, border strength, material depth, and visual priority unless the page intentionally uses a strict matrix and has another strong anchor.
- Auxiliary repeated modules should remain subordinate to the primary anchor. Reject auxiliary tags, proof points, cover benefits, or side notes that use independent podiums, large badges, strong glow, heavy 3D bases, or equal-or-greater visual weight than the main object.
- Repeated information objects retain the intended material identity. Reject pages where the model solves readability by flattening them into generic white text boxes, unless white document material was explicitly chosen as the page's main object language.
- Supporting objects visibly attach to the primary visual system through alignment, path, perspective, orbit, annotation, or note-layer logic. Reject auxiliary tags, cards, or labels that simply sit in empty space.
- The active body composition uses the canvas well after the title overlay. Reject pages with a broad inactive upper-middle region when the main modules could be higher, larger, or supported by meaningful topic context.
- Main evidence, process, or capability modules should not sit unnecessarily low. If moving them upward would improve balance without causing title collision, treat the current layout as a visual weakness and revise before accepting.
- Major visual zones share a clear layout relationship. Reject pages where auxiliary information, legends, callouts, side modules, secondary panels, evidence groups, process notes, or summary strips appear placed only to fill space rather than aligned to the primary content system.
- If a page uses a timeline, dates, node markers, connectors, and related cards or objects form clear one-to-one groups. Reject timelines where nodes hover ambiguously or the viewer must infer which content belongs to which date.
- The visual translation is topic-specific. Reject a page that could keep the same visual design after swapping in an unrelated topic.
- Run a relationship replaceability test: after hiding or changing the text, the composition should still suggest the same kind of argument. Reject outputs that remain plausible for any generic technology, platform, AI, broadcast, finance, or consulting topic.
- Reject decorative connectors that do not encode an actual relationship. A line, orbit, arrow, glow path, or flow should explain sequence, dependency, contrast, pressure, input/output, or evidence-to-conclusion; otherwise it is decoration and should not be accepted as information design.
- For `polished-enhanced` and `high-impact`, reject pages whose expressive mechanism is unclear. At least one content-driven mechanism such as scene depth, object metaphor, narrative path, contrast stage, evidence wall, system cockpit, material editorial treatment, or hero detail should be visible.
- Reject decoration that cannot explain its semantic role. Signal lines should show connection or flow; empty spaces should show gaps or capacity; glow should indicate screen light, signal, focus, or energy; material layers should clarify hierarchy.
- Reject creative extensions that invent facts, evidence, logos, customers, statistics, quotes, screenshots, or real-world proof not supplied by the source.
- If a preferred reference was used, the result borrows principles rather than copying motifs. Reject pages where the most memorable visual shape, scene, metaphor, or decorative language appears lifted from the reference while only the text changed.
- Material contrast and semantic background support the message. Reject same-color card grids, generic gradient-only backgrounds, and decorative texture without topic meaning.
- Reject pages where the dominant palette is visible only as one broad background tone plus one repeated accent tone, with no meaningful third brightness step.
- Color choices feel topic-specific and taste-aware. Reject default AI-looking blue-purple neon only when it is automatic, full-page, same-strength, or decorative without a named role; do not reject role-based blue, cyan, or blue-purple used for screen light, video signal, system connection, night-event depth, product illumination, or spatial depth. Also reject overcorrection that removes useful cold screen/video atmosphere from topics that need it.
- Reject cyan + orange when it appears to be a habitual technology/business template rather than a topic-specific decision. Also reject decks where every page repeats the same cyan background light, cyan icon/heading/border treatment, and orange highlight behavior.
- When WebGradients is used, reject outputs where the gradient is only decorative, candy-like, overly pastel, or unrelated to the topic. A passing WebGradients page should turn the selected gradient into atmosphere, material depth, local light, and hierarchy while preserving information clarity.
- Reject gradient use that is technically present but functionally empty, such as a full-page wash that does not help depth, surface separation, or local highlight logic.
- If an external color library is used, reject outputs that copy the library's UI component language without reason: filled dashboard headers, admin cards, tabs, status chips, or product-interface grouping on a presentation page that needs narrative composition.
- Reject flat same-family color use where the page has only one or two repeated colors and lacks tonal depth, material separation, local highlights, or meaningful surface hierarchy. A same-family palette should still show multiple values, intensities, and material roles.
- Reject same-family pages where fewer than three meaningful brightness levels can be identified across background, content carriers, and emphasized elements.
- Reject same-family pages where fewer than two distinct material/surface levels can be identified, such as background only plus flat cards with no surface-role distinction.
- Reject over-colorful semantic signaling where risk, opportunity, warning, success, and category colors all compete on one dense page. Prefer tonal hierarchy unless each status color has an essential role.
- Reject visual simplifications that remove or collapse required modules from the source outline. If market judgment, demand list, capability groups, or comparison modules are required, they must remain visible with improved shell quality rather than disappear for cleanliness.
- Repeated modules have internal hierarchy. Reject modules where background, border, icon, heading, body text, key number, decoration, and glow all use the same hue and brightness.
- Same-material repeated modules still separate icon, heading, body text, border, and glow. Reject modules where shared material language collapses into same-color same-intensity icon/text treatment, unless one combined mark is explicitly intended and documented.
- Reject pages where emphasis color has spread to borders, icons, labels, connectors, glow, and key numbers at the same visible intensity, because the page then loses both hierarchy and tonal depth.
- On product, parameter, and capability pages, check whether icon color and small-heading color create a clear system layer distinct from key-number emphasis. Prefer restrained cool icon/small-heading treatment with warm emphasis reserved for key values when that matches the deck direction. Reject pages where good layout is accepted despite weaker local color hierarchy if a prior candidate demonstrated a better icon/title color treatment that can be transferred.
- Repeated modules have controlled visual weight. Reject secondary objects whose borders, icons, glow, and text scale make them compete with the primary visual anchor.
- Check for role inversion: supporting modules should not be brighter, more complex, more dimensional, or more attention-grabbing than the intended S/A-level subject.
- Check real-image dominance. If a product image is marked `medium-anchor`, it may be visually present but should not outrank the primary reading target. If it is marked `evidence-schematic`, it should remain recognizable but compact and subordinate. Reject pages that promote a supporting real image into the de facto hero without user approval.
- Headings and body text have distinct treatment. Reject pages where module headings are not separated from body copy by weight, brightness, spacing, label treatment, or restrained decoration.
- Body-text carrier pages use true body-copy hierarchy: medium text scale, readable line spacing, adequate column width, and local keyword emphasis. Reject oversized paragraph typography that crowds out required information or competes with the main visual.
- The source outline's first-level information groups have visible destinations. Reject pages that silently drop required sections such as application scenarios, definitions, core functions, conclusions, or user-highlighted groups when the page is meant to preserve them.
- If `required_section_titles` exists, every required title has a visible destination as a section label or equivalent group heading. Reject pages that preserve some body text but silently omit required headings such as product definition, application scenarios, or core functions.
- Visual intensity follows the intended S/A/B/C/D scale. Reject pages where every card, icon, border, label, and connector glows at the same strength.
- Anti-AI correction is balanced. Reject both generic AI tropes and overcorrected visuals that remove useful atmosphere, lighting, depth, or modernity.
- Blue is evaluated by fit, not banned by default. If blue is used, check whether it has a clear topic, brand, product, or evidence rationale and enough secondary contrast to avoid sameness.
- For dark AI or technology pages, blue-cyan light should organize intelligence, data flow, system depth, or product illumination. Reject pages where blue is merely a decorative wash across every object.
- Page-level color roles remain varied but coherent across the deck. Reject decks where every page has the same accent spread, same card color, and same glow behavior.
- The quiet title area remains visually alive without competing with the title. Reject both title collisions and large meaningless blank patches.
- Reject any empty frame, corner bracket, panel outline, placeholder box, divider line, UI container, glow border, or decorative stroke in or visually attached to the local title area.
- Closure strips, bottom formulas, and summary callouts do not overpower the main visual system. Reject pages where the bottom closure is the strongest object while the actual evidence or capability modules feel secondary.
- Bottom conclusions are closure, not second titles. Reject pages where the bottom conclusion uses title-like scale, brightness, or glow and becomes the first or second thing the viewer reads before the primary content.
- Foreground, midground, and background have a clear relationship when the page role benefits from spatial depth.
- Blur or mentally hide the body text: the remaining image should still suggest the topic through its scene, proof object, or visual system.
- Blur or mentally hide the body text: the remaining image should also suggest the page's information relationship, not only the industry setting. If the result is merely "a nice technology scene", regenerate with a stronger visual argument grammar.
- For main repeated-module pages, the blur test must still show why the modules belong together. If the result is only several neat rectangles, regenerate with a stronger shared system anchor or second layer.
- For auxiliary repeated-module pages, the blur test should still show one dominant primary anchor with quiet support. If the auxiliary group reads as a second hero, reduce its weight.
- Modules align, supporting elements belong to clear groups, and important objects respect safe bounds.
- The title area stays clear after overlay.
- No body border, section label, icon, bright line, screenshot edge, or foreground object is visually attached to the title overlay area. Treat any collision as a high-impact failure and regenerate the body image.
- Major-zone alignment is a high-impact layout gate. Reject final samples where intended sibling zones have different outer left/right edges, arbitrary widths, or floating independent containers when the guide specified a shared parent frame. Do not show such samples for review; revise the prompt with explicit shared bounds and regenerate first.
- Main-content comfort is a high-impact layout gate. Reject pages where the main matrix, comparison table, architecture, roadmap, or workflow is visibly cramped, has overly tight line spacing, or loses readable hierarchy because title safety or auxiliary content consumed too much space. Passing title safety and alignment is not enough.
- Anti-flattening is a high-impact information-design gate. Reject pages that satisfy module count and text inclusion but feel flat, overfilled, or merely tiled, especially when a richer composition such as product anchor + function list was requested or implied.
- The title treatment fits the page role and body composition. Reject decoration that feels arbitrary, too heavy, or visually disconnected from the page.
- Do not accept a page merely because it is clean, aligned, and compliant. Reject over-corrected pages that become flat, repetitive, oversized, or template-like after detail removal.

If a high-impact item fails, load `references/failure-repair.md`, revise the prompt, and regenerate that page before continuing.
